Why elbows are important

Functionally, your elbow is a hinge with a twist—allowing flexion/extension and facilitating forearm rotation as part of the elbow complex. That range of motion positions your hand in space for precise tasks and meaningful strength.

  • Enables eating, grooming, and self‑care
  • Supports work tasks like typing and tool use
  • Allows athletic skills from throwing to lifting
  • Absorbs and redirects forces to protect the wrist and shoulder
  • Critical for balance strategies during trips and slips

Five fun facts

It’s a team effort

The “elbow” is a complex of joints—humeroulnar, humeroradial, and the proximal radioulnar—working together for hinge and rotational motion.

The funny bone isn’t a bone

That zing when you bump your elbow? It’s the ulnar nerve running through the cubital tunnel.

High‑performance hardware

Throwing and lifting can generate forces several times body weight—your elbow’s ligaments and muscles do serious work.

Precision positioning

Elbow flexion positions your hand for fine motor control—typing, drawing, and instrument playing all depend on it.

Ergonomics matter

Desk setups that keep elbows near 90–100° with supported forearms can reduce strain during computer work.
